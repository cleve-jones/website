Thirteen other takes on the mark

The wordmark is settled. This sheet is only about the symbol beside it, and it exists because of one real risk: four squares in a block with a stitched patch inside reads as a quilt organisation’s logo. The Quilt is cared for by the National AIDS Memorial. If a donor confuses the two, the foundation loses the credit for its own work.

Each set below takes a different amount of distance from that grid, from a single colour swap to leaving the grid behind. Switch the ground under every mark with the strip below, and look at the 28-pixel version in each caption: a mark that only works large is not finished.

Where this lands

What I would put in front of Cleve

The grid is the problem, and the lavender swaps do not fix it. Recolouring one tile changes the colour of the resemblance, not the resemblance — it is still four squares in a block with a stitched patch in one of them. Set 1 is here so the option is on the record, but it is the weakest answer to the question Kevin raised.

The strongest answers keep every one of the four programs and still could not be mistaken for a quilt. Option 11 does it by straightening the grid into a row: identical tiles, identical icons, no block. Option 13 does it by making the bullhorn the mark and the other three programs the sound coming out of it.

If the foundation would rather own one symbol outright, the bullhorn is the only candidate. It is Cleve’s own instrument, it is already the colour of giving across the site, and it is the one icon of the four that does not borrow its meaning from another organisation. Options 4 to 10 are all versions of that bet.

The hand-drawn set is where this could go wrong. A drawn line reads as warm and human at 150 pixels and as a smudge in an email signature. Judge those three on the small square in the caption, not the big one.

My three, in order

13 — Bullhorn, four voices
Keeps all four colours and all three programs, reads as advocacy first, and nothing about it suggests a quilt. Survives favicon size.
11 — The row
The smallest change that actually works: same tiles, same icons, same colours, straightened out. The least to approve and the least to explain.
06 — Bullhorn, no frame
If the foundation wants one symbol rather than a set. Cheapest to reproduce, hardest to get wrong, furthest from any other AIDS organisation’s mark.

Two things I need from you

Is lavender right? The shade here is a proposal, not a decision — a muted violet picked to sit beside the terracotta and sage rather than shout over them. Lavender carries its own history in this movement, from the Lavender Scare to the Lavender Menace, so it is not a neutral choice and should be a deliberate one.

Should the Quilt icon stay in the mark at all? Quilt work is one of three programs, but it is the one that borrows another organisation’s symbol. Taking it out of the logo would not take it out of the work.
